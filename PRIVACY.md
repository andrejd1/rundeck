# RunDeck for Garmin — Privacy Statement

Effective 9 October 2026

RunDeck is a data field for Garmin running activities, made by Thien Long Ngo (andrejd1),
who is responsible for the processing described here. This statement explains what data
RunDeck uses, where it stays and what, if anything, leaves your devices.

**In short:** your activity data never leaves your watch. RunDeck has no accounts, no
analytics, no ads and no tracking. The only thing it sends over the internet is your
license key, to Polar, when you unlock it.

## What RunDeck uses on your watch

To draw its screen during an activity, RunDeck reads from your watch:

- **Activity data** from the running activity: heart rate, pace, speed, distance, time,
  cadence, altitude, ascent and descent, calories, training effect, distance to a course
  destination and, if the watch has it, power.
- **Your heart-rate zones** and **running VO2 max** from your Garmin user profile.
- **The watch's temperature sensor history** (the latest reading), only when a spot shows
  temperature.
- **Sunrise and sunset** for the place where your watch last got weather, only when a spot
  shows them. RunDeck does not ask for GPS access.
- **The battery level and the time of day.**

This data is used only while the data field runs. It is not stored and never leaves the
watch. Lap values are the watch's own totals taken at each lap mark; they are kept for
the current activity only and discarded when it ends.

RunDeck keeps on the watch (Connect IQ app storage and app settings):

- your screen layout, target, zone and display settings, and up to four presets;
- the trial counter: how many runs have been counted and the start time of the last one,
  so a run is never counted twice;
- your license key, its activation id at Polar, and whether RunDeck is unlocked.

They are deleted when you remove RunDeck from the watch.

## Garmin Connect

The RunDeck settings in the Garmin Connect app (or Garmin Express) show and change the
settings kept on the watch, including the license key and its status line. Garmin moves
them between phone and watch as part of Connect IQ, so Garmin's own privacy policy also
applies to them.

## What is sent over the internet

Only license checks, and only after you enter a key. The watch sends, through your phone's
connection, to Polar (polar.sh), RunDeck's reseller:

- **Activation** (after you enter a key): the key, RunDeck's seller id at Polar, and a
  label made of `RunDeck Garmin` plus the first 8 characters of the anonymous identifier
  Connect IQ gives RunDeck on your watch, so you can tell your activations apart.
- **Validation** (at most once a week while unlocked): the key and its activation id.
- **Deactivation** (when you clear or replace the key): the old key and its activation id.

Like any web request, these also reveal your phone's IP address to Polar, and Garmin's
phone app relays them. Nothing else is sent — no activity data, no location, no settings.
If you never enter a key, RunDeck sends nothing at all.

## Buying RunDeck

Polar (polar.sh) sells RunDeck as merchant of record: it processes the payment, handles
VAT and emails you the license key. Polar collects what it needs for that (such as your
e-mail address, name, country and payment details) under
[Polar's privacy policy](https://polar.sh/legal/privacy-policy). Card details are never
shared with the developer. The developer receives the order details Polar shares with
sellers (such as e-mail address, country and the key), and uses them only to deliver and
support your license and to keep the records tax law requires.

## Your rights

Under the GDPR you can ask what data about you the developer holds, and ask for it to be
corrected or deleted. Because RunDeck keeps your data on your own devices, you control
most of it directly:

- Remove RunDeck from the watch to delete everything it stores there.
- Clear the license key in the settings to deactivate it at Polar.
- For purchase records, contact the developer or Polar.

You can also complain to your data protection authority.

## Children

RunDeck is not directed at children and does not knowingly collect data about them.
